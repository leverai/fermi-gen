## **Your Mission**

You will be asked to generate a batch of **high-quality, diverse, and delightfully engaging** Fermi Problems for a trivia game that's competing for users' attention in a crowded app market. You will be given a "seed" to spark your creativity. Each question should be a mini-adventure in reasoning that leaves players feeling clever and entertained.

## **What is a Fermi Question?**

A Fermi Question is an estimation puzzle that rewards logical thinking over memorization. The magic isn't in knowing the exact answer—it's in the satisfying journey of breaking down the problem using creative reasoning, educated guesses, and back-of-the-napkin math.

**Think:** "How many piano tuners are in NYC?" not "What is the population of NYC?"

## **The Game & Audience**

- **Target Users:** Adults (18-45) seeking casual, intellectually stimulating entertainment
- **Primary Regions:** Americas, Europe, Australia (English-speaking)
- **Mindset:** They want to feel smart without feeling stressed. Fun > Facts.
- **Success Metric:** Questions that make users think "That's such a cool question!" and keep playing

## **The 7 Golden Rules**

### **Rule 1: MUST Be Estimable (Not Just Trivia)**

Every question must be solvable through a logical chain of reasoning. If someone can just Google the exact answer, it's trivia, not a Fermi Question.

- ❌ **BAD:** "What is the population of France?" (Simple lookup)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many pizzas are eaten in France every day?" (Requires estimation chain: population → eating frequency → pizzas per meal)

### **Rule 2: MUST Be Engaging & Shareable**

The "wow factor" is critical. Questions should spark curiosity, surprise, or delight. Ask yourself: "Would someone share this with a friend?"

**What makes questions engaging:**
- Pop culture references ("How many hours of Netflix are watched globally each day?")
- Surprising scales ("How many atoms of gold are in all the wedding rings in the US?")
- Everyday mysteries ("How many pigeons are in New York City?")
- Fun juxtapositions ("How many Olympic swimming pools could you fill with all the coffee consumed in the US each year?")

- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many monks live across all monasteries in Asia?" (Intriguing, unexpected)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many hairs are on all the cats in the United States?" (Fun, relatable)
- ❌ **BORING:** "How many hospitals are in California?" (Dry, no emotional hook)

### **Rule 3: MUST Be Reasonably Answerable**

Avoid impossible estimations that would stump even experts, or questions based on myths/unknowable facts.

- ❌ **BAD:** "How many apples fell on Isaac Newton's head?" (Myth, not real data)
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many grains of sand are on all Asian beaches combined?" (Too vast, no reasonable estimation path)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many grains of sand would fill a standard sandbox?" (Specific, estimable)

**Avoid Trivial Complications:**
Don't add unnecessary arithmetic that doesn't enhance the puzzle.

- ❌ **BAD:** "How many legs are on all the spiders in Australia?" (Just multiplying spiders × 8)
- ✅ **BETTER:** "How many spiders are in Australia right now?"

### **Rule 4: MUST Be Crystal Clear**

Ambiguity kills engagement. Every question should have ONE reasonable interpretation.

**Be Specific Enough:**
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many marbles can fit inside a swimming pool" (What size pool?)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many marbles can fit inside an olympic swimming pool?"
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many 2.56 inch circular marbles can fit inside a 256x412x12 foot swimming pool?" (too specific, too dry, too boring.)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many marbles can fit inside an olympic swimming pool?"

**Provide Context:**
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many people watched the last episode of Friends?" (Where? Live only? Streaming?)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many people watched the last episode of Friends when it first aired in the US?"

**Eliminate Fluff:**
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many stars were visible to the naked eye for the ancient Babylonians?" (The "ancient Babylonians" part adds nothing)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many stars are visible to the naked eye on a clear night?"

**No Trick Questions:**
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many stars could fit inside the Great Pyramid of Giza?" (Answer: 0, it's a gotcha)

### **Rule 5: MUST Be Accessible & Relatable**

Questions should engage 80%+ of your audience, not just niche enthusiasts.

**Avoid Obscure Topics:**
- ❌ **BAD:** "How many atoms of boron are in a sheet of borosilicate glass?" (Too technical)
- ✅ **GOOD:** "How many atoms are in a grain of sand?" (Same concept, more relatable)

**Use Culturally Familiar References:**
- ⚠️ **OKAY:** "How much water flows through the Ganges River each year?" (Less familiar to Western audience)
- ✅ **BETTER:** "How much water flows through the Amazon River each year?" (Globally recognized)

**Units Matter:**
- **Never specify the unit in the question** (let the system handle it)
  - ❌ **BAD:** "How many gallons of water flow from Niagara Falls per minute?"
  - ✅ **GOOD:** "How much water flows from Niagara Falls per minute?"
- **Avoid units unfamiliar to large audience segments**
  - ❌ **BAD:** "How many people live within 100 kilometers of the Himalayas?" (Many US users don't think in km)
  - ✅ **GOOD:** "How many people live within 60 miles of the Himalayas?" OR avoid distance entirely

### **Rule 6: MUST Be Safe & Appropriate**

- No profanity, sexual content, violence, racism, or sensitive political topics
- Aim for content that would comfortably appear in a mainstream newspaper or family-friendly podcast

### **Rule 7: MUST Fit the Answer Range**

- The correct answer must be between **1 and 999 quadrillion**
- If your question's answer falls outside this range, reframe the question


## **Question Diversity & Creativity**

Each batch should include variety across the following dimensions, inspired by the provided "seed".

**Topic Categories:**
- 🌍 **Nature & Animals:** "How many ants are alive on Earth right now?"
- 🍔 **Food & Consumption:** "How many Big Macs are sold at McDonald's each year worldwide?"
- 🎬 **Pop Culture & Entertainment:** "How many hours of content are uploaded to YouTube every day?"
- 🏙️ **Urban & Infrastructure:** "How many stop signs are there in the United States?"
- 🔬 **Science & Scale:** "How many cells are in the human body?"
- 💰 **Economics & Business:** "How much does the entire fleet of Amazon delivery trucks weigh?"
- 🏃 **Human Behavior:** "How many steps does the average American take in a lifetime?"

**Scale Variety:**
- Mix small-scale ("How many words are in an average novel?")
- Mid-scale ("How many streetlights are in London?")
- Large-scale ("How many stars are visible from Earth with a telescope?")

**Creative Angles:**
- Use surprising comparisons: "How many grains of rice would it take to equal the weight of the Statue of Liberty?"
- Blend timescales: "per second," "per year," "in a lifetime"
- Ask about aggregations: "all the coffee in...", "total hours of..."

### ✅ **Excellent Questions (Aim for this quality)**

1. **"How many piano tuners are there in NYC?"**
   - ✓ Classic Fermi problem
   - ✓ Requires multi-step reasoning
   - ✓ Engaging and famous for a reason

2. **"How much do all the automobiles scrapped in North America weigh each month?"**
   - ✓ Unique, thought-provoking
   - ✓ Good estimation pathway
   - ✓ Fun fact quality

3. **"How many diapers are sold yearly in Europe?"**
   - ✓ Relatable, practical
   - ✓ Clear estimation chain: babies → usage per baby

4. **"How many monks live across all monasteries in Asia?"**
   - ✓ Surprising, culturally interesting
   - ✓ Makes you wonder

5. **"How many atoms of carbon are in a human body?"**
   - ✓ Mind-blowing scale
   - ✓ Educational and fun

### ❌ **Bad Questions (Avoid these patterns)**

1. **"How many people live within 100 kilometers of the Himalayas?"**
   - ✗ Uses kilometers (not universally intuitive)

2. **"How many grains of sand are on all Asian beaches combined?"**
   - ✗ Impossibly vast, no reasonable estimation path

3. **"How many stars were visible to the naked eye for the ancient Babylonians?"**
   - ✗ Adds confusing, irrelevant historical context

4. **"How many apples fell on Isaac Newton's head?"**
   - ✗ Based on myth, not factual

5. **"How many stars could fit inside the Great Pyramid of Giza?"**
   - ✗ Trick question (answer is 0)

### ⚠️ **Fixable Questions (Before & After)**

**Example 1:**
- ⚠️ **BEFORE:** "How much water flows through the Ganges River each year?"
- ✅ **AFTER:** "How much water flows through the Amazon River each year?"
- **Why:** Amazon is more globally recognized

**Example 2:**
- ⚠️ **BEFORE:** "How many iron atoms are in the blood of all humans combined?"
- ✅ **AFTER:** "How many iron atoms are in an average person's blood?"
- **Why:** Avoid trivial multiplication, focus on the core estimation

**Example 3:**
- ⚠️ **BEFORE:** "How long would it take to drive to the moon?"
- ✅ **AFTER:** "How long would it take to drive to the moon at highway speed?"
- **Why:** More specific, removes ambiguity

**Example 4:**
- ⚠️ **BEFORE:** "How many legs are on all the spiders in Australia?"
- ✅ **AFTER:** "How many spiders are in Australia right now?"
- **Why:** The "legs" part is just multiplying by 8—adds no value
